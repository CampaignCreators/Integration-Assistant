SAMPLE FILE — fictional company, invented for testing the intake flow.
Nothing in here is a real client, a real person, or a real requirement.

Discovery call — Northwind Plumbing & HVAC
Attendees: Dana (Campaign Creators), Marcus (Northwind, Ops Manager), Priya (Northwind, Sales)
Duration: 34 min

---

Dana: Thanks for making time. Before we get into the weeds — tell me what a job
looks like today, from the first phone call to the invoice going out.

Marcus: Sure. A call comes in, the front desk takes it, and they create the
customer in ServiceTitan. That's our field service system — dispatch,
scheduling, invoicing, all of it lives there. Then if it's a bigger quote, Priya
picks it up on the sales side.

Priya: And that's where it falls apart, honestly. Everything I do lives in
HubSpot. So when the front desk creates a customer in ServiceTitan, I don't
know about it unless someone tells me. I end up typing the same customer into
HubSpot by hand — name, email, phone, address. Every single time.

Dana: How many of those a week, roughly?

Priya: Forty? Fifty on a busy week. And I'm sure I fat-finger some of them.

Marcus: We caught one last month where the email had a typo, so the customer
never got the estimate follow-up. That's a real job we lost.

Dana: Understood. So the first thing is customer records flowing from
ServiceTitan into HubSpot. What about the other direction — does anything need
to go back?

Priya: I'd love it if the estimate status came back. When I mark a deal as
Closed Won in HubSpot, someone still has to go create the job in ServiceTitan.

Marcus: I'd push back a little there. I don't want HubSpot writing into
ServiceTitan yet. Dispatch is the one thing that can't break — if a bad record
creates a phantom job, we've got a truck driving to nowhere. Read-only into
HubSpot first, and we can talk about writing back later.

Priya: That's fair. Start one direction.

Dana: Good, that's a clear scope line. Now, when a customer exists in both
systems, how do you know it's the same customer?

Priya: Email, mostly. Although a lot of the residential ones give us a phone
number and no email at all.

Marcus: The commercial accounts always have email. Residential is maybe 70%.

Dana: That's worth flagging — we'll need a fallback when there's no email.
What about jobs and estimates? Do those need to show up in HubSpot too?

Priya: Jobs would be useful. If I can see that a customer had three service
calls this year, that changes how I talk to them about a maintenance plan.

Dana: How current does that need to be? Live, or is nightly enough?

Priya: Same day is fine for job history. But a new customer I'd want quickly —
within the hour, ideally, because I'm often calling them back that afternoon.

Dana: Noted — near-real-time for customers, nightly is fine for job history.
Roughly how many customer records are in ServiceTitan today?

Marcus: Somewhere around 12,000. We've been on it four years.

Dana: And do you have API access on your ServiceTitan plan? Some tiers don't.

Marcus: I genuinely don't know. Our account manager mentioned an integrations
add-on at some point. I'd have to ask.

Dana: That's fine — we'll research it and confirm with you. Last thing: is
there anyone technical on your side who'd own this?

Marcus: Not really. We've got an IT contractor who does maybe four hours a
month. Whatever we build, we can't be maintaining it ourselves.

Dana: Then low-maintenance matters more than clever. Let me go do the research
and come back with options.

Marcus: One more thing — we're mid-season. Whatever this is, it can't take the
system down during the day.
